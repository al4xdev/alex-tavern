# Reading the conversation before choosing another metric

2026-09-05. Two isolated readers received only continuous fiction excerpts: narration, speech and actions, with canonical speaker labels. Neither received code, the roadmap, earlier findings, scores or the hypothesis under investigation. Each read turns 5–12 from one of the two archived post-79 sessions, ea6620fb and bb72dc94, through the records visible to Link. Private thoughts and other audiences were excluded. The fixed window was selected before their readings; it was not a sample of detector hits. This is a scoped qualitative read, not an A/B or a corpus estimate.

## What the text readers found

**OBSERVED, ea6620fb.** The reader found concrete atmosphere, differentiated voices and actual progression: injury, evacuation, a crossing and a blocked route. Bruna's “o ombro pode esperar, a viga não” answers the immediate danger effectively. The same reader identified dialogue that fails to recognize completed action: Link “acompanha o movimento da fila”, then Marta “guia-o pela passagem”, yet later dialogue keeps threatening to drag him if he will not move. Repeated exhortation can be characterization; here the reader could not reconcile it with the already narrated movement.

The final crossing is harder to follow: the beam blocks the corridor “atrás do grupo”, after Marta has been guiding Link, but Asword says “A viga caiu e separou Link do grupo”. Seraphine accepts that as possible while Marta still addresses Link as accompanying her. The reader cannot confidently place Link. This supports a reader-facing continuity ambiguity, not proof that the physical state teleported him or that a character cannot be mistaken. The phrase “the group” itself leaves membership implicit.

**OBSERVED, bb72dc94.** The reader found a legible institutional conflict and useful character decisions, particularly Nix's concern about tools versus lineage and the later distribution of evacuation roles. The messenger sequence nevertheless reverses an established action. Garran takes and reads the scroll, “se põe de pé”, and discusses its contents. The next narration has the scroll escape the messenger's fingers and Garran “ainda de joelhos”. A return of the paper and another kneeling action could explain this, but neither is supplied between these events. This is more specific than calling the scene repetitive.

The same reader points to the students already “divididos em grupos de quatro” and moving toward the rear exit, followed by students “imóveis em suas marcas” and renewed demands to form quartets. The reader distinguishes an imaginable hesitation from the text restarting an already described stage of the evacuation. The reader treats recurring dust, alarms and lamps as a stylistic reservation, not automatically a logical defect.

These are two readers of different excerpts, not independent agreement on the same evidence. They did not conclude that nothing happens, that explicit movement is always desirable, or that all repeated speech is defective.

## Consequence for evaluation

Retain the old movement judge as a rejected acceptance instrument, with its failure history. Do not replace its unknown percentage with a fluency score merely because a reader can produce one. These readings support assessing a complete visible sequence: can a reader follow what changed, who has acted, and whether the following dialogue recognizes that change? Elided movement is acceptable when the sequence remains understandable. A precise but unsupported crossing is not an improvement.

This is a proposed qualitative acceptance question, not a validated new metric or a production acceptance result. A future paired comparison still needs matched inputs, concealed variants, contextual citations and attention to both improvements and harms. It must also allow “no preference” and insufficient evidence. The supplied readings assess the shipped scene, without establishing whether blocking improved it. These readings do not close quality acceptance. I will cross-reference the observations in the existing continuity investigation (69), narration/state hypothesis (81) and blocking evaluation (79); that is a scope decision, not a result measured by the readers. This work makes no schema change.

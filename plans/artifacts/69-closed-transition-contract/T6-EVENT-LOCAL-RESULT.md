# T6 event-local reader-unit screen: readable parts, incomplete whole

The frozen [protocol](T6-EVENT-LOCAL-PREREGISTRATION.md) and
`t6-event-local-manifest.json` selected the accepted `d5a2ccf0` T6
Director events and the actual origin-viewer prose request. Three independent
DeepSeek calls rendered one confirmed event each; their outputs were joined
in event order. Four independent repetitions made **12 direct `curl` calls**.
All 12 returned HTTP 200, distinct response IDs and valid `{"unit": string}`
objects. Raw envelopes, exact requests, hashes, outputs and assembled texts
are preserved in `t6-event-local-runs/`. This is a technically complete
**one-episode** screen, not a production reliability rate.

The named Garran crossing survives in all four assemblies, and none opens
with the orphan `ele` left by T6's current post-render filter. An independent
fiction reader read these four assemblies and found three flowing, with the
third opening like a report. A separate earlier reader found the unchanged
event concatenation too report-like; those are **different readers**, so this
is not a controlled prose-quality improvement. The assembly reader also
noticed reuse of dust, lamps and stone across independently written
paragraphs. One concrete example is assembly 4: the first paragraph says
`A poeira ainda paira`, and the next begins `A poeira ainda flutua`.

The **registered content gate fails** for reasons readable without a metric.
Assembly 2 drops the confirmed several-metre roll of the falling block:
`quicando duas vezes sobre o entulho antes de parar de lado` ends the motion
on the rubble instead. Assembly 4 omits that Elowen's murmuring is a
**stabilization prayer**, despite that being explicit in its isolated source
event and assigned to origin witnesses by the accepted Director record.
The block falls near the origin audience's obstruction, so neither missing
fact can be dismissed as destination-only information. Assembly 1 says
Garran's silhouette disappears into the corridor,
then gives him another visible action there: `Ele firma os pés no chão coberto
de detritos`. The source confirms his crossing and positioning, so planting
his feet could be interpreted as the end of that same move, but the sentence
comes **after** the text says he disappeared from the origin view; this is at
least a viewpoint ambiguity, not evidence of a new consequential action.
The reader also questioned destination-side detail in assembly 3, though
the accepted event itself reports the partially obstructed corridor to the
origin witnesses; that ambiguity is not counted as a separate failure.

The crossing note was **manually** matched to Garran's accepted move; this
screen did not test automatic binding from `subject_id=Narrator` to
`zone_moves.C18`.
No destination-view projection, incoming mover, or offstage negative control
was run. This candidate is **not admitted**, and no runtime code changed.
The four assembled texts and reader findings are local evidence for revising
the unit interface or testing a different assembly, not grounds to reject
event-local narration in general.
